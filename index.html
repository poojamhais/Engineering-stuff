What is Ohm's Law?

Ohm's Law is the most fundamental relationship in electrical engineering. Discovered by Georg Simon Ohm in 1827, it describes how voltage, current, and resistance are interrelated in an electrical circuit.

V = I × R
⚡

Voltage (V)

The electrical pressure that pushes electrons through a conductor. Measured in Volts (V).

🌊

Current (I)

The rate of flow of electric charge through a conductor. Measured in Amperes (A).

🔥

Resistance (R)

The opposition to the flow of current in a circuit. Measured in Ohms (Ω).

The Basic Circuit

A simple circuit consists of a voltage source (battery), a load (resistor), and connecting wires. Current flows from the positive terminal through the circuit and back to the negative terminal.

🔋

Voltage Source

Provides the electromotive force (EMF) to drive current through the circuit.

📐

Resistor

Opposes current flow. Color bands indicate its resistance value in Ohms.

Key Insight: If you increase the voltage while keeping resistance constant, the current increases proportionally. This linear relationship is the essence of Ohm's Law.

The Water Analogy

The easiest way to understand Ohm's Law is by comparing electricity to water flowing through pipes.

Voltage = Water Pressure

Like water pressure pushing water through a pipe, voltage pushes electrons through a wire.

Current = Water Flow Rate

The amount of water flowing per second is like current — the amount of charge flowing per second.

Resistance = Pipe Width

A narrow pipe restricts water flow, just as high resistance restricts electric current.

Solved Examples

Practice problems for first-year engineering students.

Example 1: Finding Current

Given: V = 24V, R = 8Ω

Using Ohm's Law: I = V / R = 24 / 8

Answer: I = 3 Amperes

Example 2: Finding Voltage

Given: I = 5A, R = 12Ω

Using Ohm's Law: V = I × R = 5 × 12

Answer: V = 60 Volts

Example 3: Finding Resistance

Given: V = 120V, I = 0.5A

Using Ohm's Law: R = V / I = 120 / 0.5

Answer: R = 240 Ohms

Limitations of Ohm's Law

Ohm's Law applies to ohmic (linear) conductors under constant physical conditions. It does not hold for all materials.

  • ⚠️ Non-linear devices — Diodes, transistors, and LEDs do not obey Ohm's Law as their V-I relationship is non-linear.
  • ⚠️ Temperature dependence — Resistance of most conductors changes with temperature. Ohm's Law assumes constant temperature.
  • ⚠️ High-frequency AC circuits — Impedance (not just resistance) must be considered when capacitance and inductance are present.
  • ⚠️ Semiconductors — Materials like silicon and germanium have complex V-I characteristics that vary with doping and temperature.
  • ⚠️ Superconductors — At extremely low temperatures, some materials have zero resistance, making Ohm's Law inapplicable.
